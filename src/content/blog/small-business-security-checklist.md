Most attacks on small businesses aren't sophisticated. They're stolen passwords, fake invoices and unpatched software. The good news: a handful of basics stops most of them.

## 1. Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA) everywhere

Email, banking, accounting, social media, your website admin. If an account supports MFA, turn it on. Prefer an authenticator app or passkey over SMS codes.

## 2. Use a password manager

One strong, unique password per account. A password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password, Apple Passwords) makes this painless and stops one leaked password from unlocking everything.

## 3. Secure your email first

Your email resets every other password, so it's the master key. Give it the strongest protection, and check who else has access to it.

## 4. Keep backups — and test them

Follow the 3-2-1 rule: three copies, on two different types of storage, one kept offline or off-site. A backup you've never restored is a hope, not a backup.

## 5. Update everything, automatically

Turn on automatic updates for computers, phones, routers, website plugins and apps. Most attacks use holes that were already fixed.

## 6. Verify payment changes by phone

"We've changed our bank details" emails are one of the most expensive scams around. Always confirm by calling a number you already have — never one from the email.

## 7. Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your domain

These three DNS records stop criminals from sending email that looks like it came from your business. Your email provider has a guide; it takes about 15 minutes.

## 8. Remove access when people leave

Keep a simple list of who has access to what. When someone leaves, remove their accounts the same day.

## 9. Separate admin accounts from everyday ones

Don't browse the web or read email while logged in as an administrator. Use admin accounts only when you need them.

## 10. Know what you'd do if something went wrong

Write down, on one page: who to call, how to lock accounts, where the backups are. Panic is the enemy; a plan beats it.
